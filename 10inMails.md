### Claude Opus 5 High No Thinking

10 credits. LinkedIn gives one back whenever the person replies at all, even to say no. So optimize for reply rate, not conversion. A "not now, keep me posted" costs you nothing.

Second rule. Never spend a credit on someone you can reach by email, warm intro, or a comment on their own post. InMail is for people with no cheaper door.

4 for fractional CTO buyers who have already admitted the gap. Series A or B, B2B SaaS, 20 to 100 people, founder or CEO only. The signal isn't "posted about AI." It's an open AI or ML engineering req sitting live for 45 days or more with no CTO above it, or a first-time VP Eng. A stale req means they named the need, failed to hire, and are paying for the gap every month. Say those two sentences back to them.

2 for BLT, aimed at people mid-migration. Head of Product or Ops at payments and POS ISVs who have a date on the calendar. Processor sunset notice, PCI deadline, announced re-platform. Not "companies in payments." Companies with a deadline. BLT runs, so open with the thing, not the pitch.

2 for access brokers, not audiences. Agency and dev shop owners in your ICP are the pick here. They field AI requests every week they can't staff, and handing one to you costs them nothing. Community leads and hosts are fine, but the shop owner has a reason to answer today.

2 held back, unspent. Release only on a trigger in the next 30 days: new VP Eng, funding round, eng lead departs, someone engages with your post. A credit spent the week something changed beats one spent on a cold, static profile. Committing all ten up front is the mistake.

What I'd cut from the Muse version below: the 3 credits on Niche Product Lab. Operators with audiences are the easiest people alive to reach. Reply to the newsletter. DM them. Comment. Spending scarce InMail there wastes the only thing InMail is good for.
